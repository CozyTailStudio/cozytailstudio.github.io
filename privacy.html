Privacy Policy - Cozy Tail Studio

Effective date: 4 November 2026. Last updated: 4 November 2026

In short

  • The Game has no account. We do not know your name.
  • Your puzzle progress stays on your phone.
  • Ads come from Google AdMob. Your choice in the privacy form is about personalized ads and tracking. It does not turn ads off.
  • When you first start the Game, it asks in its own window whether it may send us game statistics and crash reports. If you say no, it sends us neither.
  • Purchases are handled by Google Play. The Game never asks for or stores your card or payment details.
  • Uninstalling the Game or using Android "Clear data" removes your progress from your phone. Settings has "Delete my data" for your statistics (shown only while statistics are on).
  • Questions about your data: info@cozytailstudio.com.

2. This website

This website does not use cookies, analytics or advertising, and has no accounts or forms. 3. Our games

Fern & Grid: Cozy Nonogram

Fern & Grid: Cozy Nonogram (the "Game") is published by us. This section describes the version we publish through Google Play. It is for people aged 16 and over.

  • No account. The Game has no account of its own. We do not ask for your name, e-mail, phone number, location, contacts, photos or birth date. There is no sign-in of any kind.
  • On your phone. Your puzzle progress, collection, settings and choices are stored on your phone. We do not receive them. If the Android backup feature is on, the save file may be copied to your own Google account; Google handles that copy under your Google account settings, not us.
  • Ads (Google AdMob). The Game shows ads: a full-screen ad between puzzles and video ads that you choose to watch. The ads are served by Google AdMob, through Google's Mobile Ads SDK (the "Next-Gen" version). No other advertising network is built into this version.
  • What AdMob may collect. When ads are shown, Google receives technical information from your device and may use it as an independent controller, under Google's own privacy policy (policies.google.com/privacy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services). This can be your IP address (which can give an approximate location); identifiers such as the Android advertising ID and the app set ID, depending on your choice and on Google's settings; ad interactions such as app launches, taps and video views; and diagnostic information such as app launch time and hang rate.
  • Why, and how to limit it. Google uses this to show and measure ads, to prevent fraud and, if you agreed, to personalize ads. You can reset or delete the advertising ID in your Android settings (usually under Google, Ads).
  • Google's consent form. Where Google's consent form applies to your location, it asks for your choice when you first start the Game, before any ad is requested. Where it applies, it comes first and our own window about statistics (described in the items below) comes after it. At every start the Game also checks, with Google's consent library, whether the form is needed; that library is run by Google. Where Google provides a way to change your choice for your region, you can open the form again under Settings, Privacy choices. Where Google's form does not apply, that row says there are no ad choices in your region.
  • What your choice does. Your choice in the form is about personalization and tracking only. It does not turn ads off, and it does not block play or change what the Game can do. If you do not consent, ads can still appear, but they are not matched to you.
  • Ads after "no". Google says these ads do not use personal data for ad personalization. Data such as your IP address is still used to deliver the ad, and Google may use cookies or local storage to detect invalid traffic.
  • Our own window about statistics. After Google's form (where that form applies), the Game shows its own window once, titled "Help us make the game better". It asks whether the Game may send game statistics and crash reports to Google (Firebase). It has the buttons "Yes, share" and "No thanks", and "More details", which shows more. We show it to everyone, in every country, and not during a puzzle. Whatever you choose, the Game plays the same. If you close the app without answering, nothing is stored and the Game asks again at the next start.
  • Before your answer. Until you answer, the Game does not send us statistics or crash reports, and no answer counts as "no". At every start, Google's consent library and Google AdMob contact Google, as described above.
  • Changing or withdrawing your answer. The only place to change your answer is the switch "Statistics and crash reports" in Settings. It is its own row, directly under the row "Privacy choices", and it works the same in every region. Google's form under "Privacy choices" is about ads only. A "no" or a switch-off clears what the Game stored for statistics on your phone (see "4. Your rights").
  • Usage statistics (Google Firebase Analytics). If you say yes, the Game sends events such as the steps of the lesson, a puzzle started, given up or finished with its size, time and number of mistakes, hints used, whether an ad was shown or watched, and whether a purchase was started or finished. Our events contain no name, e-mail or free text. Firebase can also record purchase events with the product, its name and its price for purchases made through Google Play.
  • What Firebase adds. Firebase records its own automatic events (for example first open and sessions), and the Game sets a few labels, such as whether Ad-free is active, the version of its settings and the test group. One of the labels is your Statistics ID (explained in the items on "Delete my data"), set only if you said yes. In short, the data are: a random ID, your phone model, your country, the app version and what happens in the Game. Firebase adds a random app-instance identifier and an installation identifier, the system version, a country or region taken from your IP address, and the install source (where Google Play says the install came from). We switched off Granular location and device data collection in Google Analytics, so Analytics does not record your city or detailed device data; crash reports still contain the device model. We turned off the setting with which Firebase Analytics collects the Android advertising ID; Google's ad software (AdMob) uses it for ads, as described above.
  • What we do not set. We do not set Firebase's user ID field; the Statistics ID is an ordinary label. In Google Analytics we switched off Google signals and ads personalization (for all regions) and the four optional data-sharing settings. Firebase is provided by Google, which, according to Google's Firebase privacy information, generally acts as a data processor for us (Firebase privacy information).
  • Links between Google services. We linked Firebase to Google AdMob and to Google Play. AdMob passes ad revenue figures into our statistics. Google says that when AdMob is linked, Google Analytics data is available in AdMob whatever our own Analytics data-sharing settings are, so statistics of players who said yes can reach AdMob, where Google decides about its own use under its own privacy policy. We also switched on revenue, audience and crash data sharing between Firebase and Google Play for our reports. The four optional Analytics data-sharing settings we switched off do not stop these links.
  • Crash reports (Google Firebase Crashlytics). If you say yes, the Game sends crash reports. A report contains, for example, what the Game was doing when it crashed (a stack trace), the device model, the system version, the amount of memory, session data and random installation identifiers. We add no name, e-mail, user ID or free text.
  • If you say no to crash reports. Nothing is sent, and crash reports not yet sent are deleted from your phone. Google generally acts as our data processor for Crashlytics and keeps crash reports for 90 days (Firebase privacy information).
  • Storage of the statistics (Google BigQuery, EU). We copy the raw statistics events from Firebase Analytics every day to a BigQuery dataset in our own Google Cloud project, located in Poland (Google Cloud region europe-central2, Warsaw), inside the European Union. We use Google's free BigQuery sandbox, without billing. The copy contains the same events and random identifiers, plus the fields Google adds to its export, such as country or region, language, time zone and install source. It has no names or e-mails, and only the owner of the studio can open it. Google acts as our data processor for this storage.
  • Why we keep it. We use it to look for puzzles that are too hard or too easy and for errors. Our plan is that the tables in the dataset are deleted automatically after 60 days, which is the default of the BigQuery sandbox. The sandbox has a storage limit for the life of the project; when it is reached, the daily copy stops. We do not promise that the copy runs without a break.
  • Game settings from Firebase (Remote Config). The Game can ask Firebase for a few settings, such as how often ads and offers appear. This happens only when you have said yes to statistics. To pick the settings, Firebase uses the installation identifier and receives technical data such as your country, language, time zone, system version and the name of the Game.
  • Without Remote Config. If you say no, the Game uses the settings built into it, and nothing changes in play. Settings never change what you have bought. Google generally acts as our data processor here too.
  • Statistics ID. The Game makes a random Statistics ID on your phone. It is stored only on the phone, outside the Android backup, and is never made from a device identifier. The Game sends it to Firebase Analytics as the label "statistics_id" only if you said yes. It is not your name and we cannot tell who it belongs to unless you send it to us in a request.
  • "Delete my data". This row in Settings exists only while statistics are on. Before your answer, after a "no" and after a request, there is nothing to delete and the row is not shown. Its sheet shows your Statistics ID with a button "Copy ID" and a button "Send deletion request". It is about statistics only.
  • Send deletion request. The button opens your mail app with a message to info@cozytailstudio.com, the subject "Privacy request" and your Statistics ID. If the phone has no mail app, the Game shows its Contact sheet with the ID so you can write to us yourself.
  • What the Game does at once. After you press the button, the Game stops statistics, drops the events waiting to be sent, resets the Firebase Analytics data on your phone, deletes crash reports not yet sent and its local log, forgets the stored copy of the Remote Config settings, and makes a new Statistics ID. Your puzzle progress is not touched.
  • What we do on your request. We read your e-mail and answer within one month. We use the old ID from your message to look for your installation in our statistics and to ask Google to delete its Analytics data for it. We do this by hand, so we cannot promise a date; Google deletes on its own systems, and data already at Google stays until Google has deleted it or it expires (see "How long we keep data").
  • After a request. Statistics stay off, and the Game does not turn them on again by itself.
  • Your puzzle progress. It is stored on your phone. Uninstalling the Game or using Android "Clear data" removes the data stored on your phone. If Android backup is on, a copy of the save may be kept in your own Google account and may come back when you install the Game again on this or a new phone with the same Google account. The consent state and the Statistics ID are not restored. Purchases come from Google Play, not from the backup. You can manage or delete the backup in your Google account settings. We cannot see or delete it.
  • Feedback and ratings. "Tell us what you think" in Settings opens your own e-mail app with a message addressed to us. You decide what to write and whether to send it; what you send is handled under "Answering your e-mails" in the table at the end. The Game may also show a card with a button that opens the Game's page in Google Play, where you can rate it.
  • Ratings are Google's. The page is run by Google Play. The Game does not receive your rating or whether you wrote a review, and gives nothing in return for one.
  • Purchases. In-app purchases (Ad-free, Gardens 3 to 5 and bundles) are handled by Google Play. Google handles your payment details. The Game receives the confirmation of a purchase from Google Play so it can unlock and restore it, and Google reports sales to us for our tax and accounting records.
  • What the Game keeps about purchases. The Game does not keep what you own in its save file or in your backup. It keeps a small signed cache on your phone, outside the backup, which Google Play checks again. Google Play Billing may send technical and purchase information to Google, which Google handles under its own privacy policy.
  • Google Play. If you install the Game from Google Play, Google processes your data under its own terms and privacy policy. Google also gives us technical reports, for example crash and not-responding reports from devices whose owners agreed to share diagnostics with Google, and install statistics. These reach us from Google, not from you; we read them in Google Play Console and use them only to fix problems and to check that the Game works on your kind of device.
  • What the Game does not use. The Game does not ask for your location, contacts, camera or microphone and sends no notifications.
  • Testers. If you join a closed test, we use the e-mail address you gave us only to add you to the tester list and to answer you. We remove the address from the list when the test ends and keep any e-mails you wrote to us for up to 12 months after the last message. For open testing in Google Play we keep no tester list.
  • Children. The Game is not directed to children. It is for people aged 16 and over, which includes 16 and 17 year olds. We do not knowingly collect personal data from people under 16; if you are a parent or guardian, or you believe a person under 16 has given us data, write to info@cozytailstudio.com and we will delete what we can find.
  • Automated decisions. We do not take decisions about you based only on automated processing.
  • Deletion and questions. Write to info@cozytailstudio.com with the subject "Privacy request". Uninstalling the Game or using Android "Clear data" removes the data stored on your phone. If Android backup is on, a copy in your Google account can come back when you install the Game again; Google handles that copy under your Google account settings.

How long we keep data

  • Statistics in Google Analytics: 2 months for event data and for user data, and new activity does not restart the period (we set this in Google Analytics). We keep no player profile of our own and no player account.
  • Statistics copied to BigQuery: our plan is up to 60 days, after which the tables are deleted automatically (our plan until the setting is checked in the dataset).
  • Crash reports (Crashlytics): 90 days at Google.
  • Firebase installation identifiers (used by Crashlytics and Remote Config): they stay with Google until they are deleted through Google's tools; Google removes them from its systems within 180 days after deletion. We cannot ask for their deletion on your request, see "4. Your rights".
  • Deletion at Google: if Google is asked to delete data, it can take up to 180 days to finish on its systems.
  • Data held by Google for ads, Google Play and Play Billing: under Google's own policies.
  • Statistics deletion requests: we keep the e-mail with the Statistics ID for up to 12 months after the last message, like other e-mails.
  • Crash reports and install statistics from Google Play: we read them in Google Play Console, where Google keeps them under its own rules.
  • E-mails to us: up to 12 months after the last message.
  • Closed test tester list: until the test ends.
  • Purchase and tax records: 5 years, counted from the end of the calendar year in which the purchase was made, as Polish accounting and tax rules require.

Security

We have no server of our own that stores player data. Statistics events are stored at Google (Firebase, and BigQuery in the EU), and access to our Google accounts is limited to the studio owner. According to Google's documentation, data that the AdMob and Firebase software sends is encrypted in transit.

US residents

If you live in a US state with a privacy law you may have the right to know, access, correct and delete your personal data, and to opt out of "sale", "sharing" or targeted advertising. We do not sell your data. Ad IDs go to Google AdMob to show ads, as described above, and sharing an advertising identifier with an advertising provider may count as "sharing". Use Settings, Privacy choices in the Game, where it is offered, or write to us. The Game's own switch "Statistics and crash reports" covers statistics and crash reports only.

4. Your rights

If you are in the EEA, UK or Switzerland you may ask us for access, correction, deletion, restriction or portability of your data, object to processing and withdraw consent, by writing to info@cozytailstudio.com with the subject "Privacy request". We answer within one month.

What we can find: your e-mails to us, your place on a closed test list, our purchase records, and statistics that belong to a Statistics ID you send us. We can access, correct or delete these.

How your statistics are deleted, and by whom:

  1. The Game, on your phone, at once. When you say no, or withdraw a yes, the Game stops sending statistics and crash reports, resets its local statistics identifier, drops the events waiting to be sent, deletes crash reports not yet sent and forgets the stored Remote Config settings. Ads are not switched off; limited ads remain.
  2. Data already sent stays at Google until it expires. Analytics: 2 months. BigQuery copy: up to 60 days. Crash reports: 90 days.
  3. We, on your request. Open Settings, "Delete my data", and send the e-mail with your Statistics ID (or write to us yourself with that ID). We answer within one month. We use the ID to ask Google to delete the Analytics data of your installation; Google can take up to 180 days to finish. Our BigQuery copy cannot be searched and edited row by row; it is deleted as a whole when the tables expire, at most 60 days after they are written.
  4. Request before you say no. When you say no or withdraw, the identifier on your phone is reset, so we can no longer find the statistics sent before. The row "Delete my data" is also hidden then. If you want them deleted before they expire, send the request first and say no afterwards.
  5. What we cannot delete on request. The Firebase installation identifier and the crash-report identifier are random and we cannot find them from your message. They stay at Google under its rules (see "How long we keep data").
  6. If you said no from the start, the Game sent us no statistics and no crash reports, so there is nothing of that kind to delete. Data that Google AdMob handles for ads is handled by Google.

To stop new statistics, use the switch "Statistics and crash reports" in Settings, the row directly under "Privacy choices". "Delete my data" also stops them (see section 3). Google's form (Settings, Privacy choices, where it applies) is about ads.

Data held by Google: use Google's own tools, for example your Google Account and the advertising ID settings on your phone.

You may also complain to a supervisory authority; in Poland this is the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO), ul. Stanisława Moniuszki 1A, 00-014 Warszawa, https://uodo.gov.pl. For the rules on storing or reading information on your device (Art. 399 of the Polish Electronic Communications Law), the supervisory authority in Poland is the President of the Office of Electronic Communications (Prezes UKE), ul.
